Platform Guides
1
Choose a Template
From your dashboard, tap New Resume. You'll see all 13 templates — Free plan members can build with 5 of them, Pro and Premier unlock the full set. Each template isn't just a different font — they're genuinely different layouts: some are single-column and text-first (safest for ATS parsing), some are two-column with a sidebar for skills and contact info (great visually, but worth checking your ATS score after), and a couple support a profile photo for roles where that helps. If you're not sure, tap Match Template Quiz — answer a few questions about your industry and seniority and KIE recommends one.
💡 Tip: Applying to a conservative industry (law, finance, government)? Stick to single-column templates like Classic or Elegant — recruiters there tend to expect a traditional look, and ATS software parses them more reliably.
2
Personal Info & Summary (Step 1)
Enter your full name, target job title, email, phone, and location. The job title field matters more than it looks — it's what KIE uses to tailor everything downstream. The moment you fill it in, KIE automatically generates 3 different professional summary options right there in the form — tap one to insert it, then edit it to sound like you. A strong summary is genuinely worth 15 of your ATS score's 100 points, and specifically rewards being present, being 40+ words long, and being written for your actual target role rather than generic.
3
Work Experience & Education (Step 2)
For each role, add your position, company, start/end dates, and a description. This is the single heaviest-weighted section of your ATS score — 30 of 100 points — and it's not just about having entries. Points come specifically from having real descriptions, from those descriptions containing a quantified result or metric (a number, a percentage, a naira/dollar figure), and from using strong action verbs instead of passive phrasing. "Responsible for managing a team" scores worse than "Managed a team of 6, cutting onboarding time by 35%" — same job, very different signal. Ask KIE to turn any rough bullet into a stronger one; it's built to push you toward specifics.
💡 Tip: If you genuinely can't quantify something, quantify the scale instead — team size, number of clients, budget managed, tickets resolved per week. A number is a number.
4
Skills & Save (Step 3)
Add your skills one at a time — KIE will suggest relevant ones based on your job title if you're stuck. This section rewards having 5 or more skills and a mix of technical and soft skills, not just a long list of buzzwords. Once everything looks right, name your resume (something like "Product Manager — Fintech" if you're keeping multiple versions) and tap Save Resume. Saving is the line between a "Draft" and a real, permanent resume — drafts live only in your browser and can't be downloaded as PDF until you save.
5
Check Your ATS Score, Then Download
Once saved, you'll see your ATS score immediately — every plan gets the number, Pro and Premier get the full breakdown of exactly which of the 6 scoring categories (contact info, summary, experience, education, skills, formatting) is costing you points and what to fix. When you're happy, tap Download PDF from the dashboard or the resume detail page. If nothing happens, check that your browser allows pop-ups for this site — that's the #1 cause of a PDF that "won't download."
✨ There are 13 templates in total. Free gives you 5 of them; Pro and Premier unlock all 13. Every template shares the same content — switching styles never touches your actual data.
🏛️ Classic & Elegant
Best for: Finance, Law, Government, Consulting
Single-column, traditional structure, muted colors. This is the safest style for both humans and machines — recruiters in conservative industries expect exactly this look, and single-column layouts are the easiest for ATS software to parse top-to-bottom without scrambling your section order.
⚡ Modern, Minimal & Slate
Best for: Tech, Product, Startups, Data
Clean contemporary layouts with clear section hierarchy and generous white space. Slate adds a dark accent panel for extra visual weight. These read well to both a hiring manager skimming in 7 seconds and to an ATS — the hierarchy is visual but the underlying structure stays linear.
💼 Bold & Executive
Best for: Senior, Director & Leadership roles
Heavier typography and confident spacing — designed to signal seniority at a glance. Works especially well when your work experience section is doing the heavy lifting (which, at 30 of 100 ATS points, it always should be).
🎨 Vivid, Coral & Ink
Best for: Creative, Media, Marketing, Design
Bold color and standout layout choices, built to be memorable in industries where your resume is itself a small piece of portfolio evidence. These lean more visual, so if you're applying somewhere that also runs your resume through an ATS first, pair one of these with the full ATS breakdown to double-check nothing important is getting lost in the layout.
🖼️ Nova & Tribune
Best for: Sales, Customer-facing, Personal-brand-forward roles
The two templates that support a profile photo. Useful where a face genuinely helps build trust before the first call — sales, hospitality, coaching, community roles. Skip these for roles/regions where photo-on-resume isn't the norm, since it can invite unconscious bias rather than help you.
📐 Split
Best for: General use, multi-industry applicants
A structured two-column layout that adapts well when you're applying across a few different fields at once and don't want to commit to one strong aesthetic direction.

3
Exactly how your ATS score is calculated
This is the part most people never see explained anywhere: your score out of 100 is built from 6 weighted categories. Contact info (15 pts) — name, email, phone, location all present. Professional summary (15 pts) — present, 40+ words, and written for your specific role rather than generic. Work experience (30 pts, the biggest single category) — entries present, real descriptions, at least one quantified metric per role, and strong action verbs instead of passive phrasing. Education (15 pts) — entries present with degree and field specified. Skills (15 pts) — 5 or more skills, mixing technical and soft skills rather than just one type. Formatting signals (10 pts) — LinkedIn or portfolio link present, consistent date formatting, no obvious errors. Add it up and you get a grade: A+ (90+), A (80-89), B+ (75-79), B (65-74), C+ (55-64), C (45-54), or D (under 45).
💡 Because Work Experience alone is worth 30 points — more than Education and Skills combined — the single highest-leverage thing you can do for your score is go back through your bullet points and add one real number to each.
